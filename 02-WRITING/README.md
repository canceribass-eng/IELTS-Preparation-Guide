"The graph below shows the percentage of population living in urban areas in different regions between 2000 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features."

**Grafik Verisi:**
- 2000: Asia 35%, Europe 72%, Africa 36%, Americas 78%
- 2010: Asia 42%, Europe 74%, Africa 38%, Americas 81%
- 2020: Asia 52%, Europe 77%, Africa 44%, Americas 84%

### 🟡 ORTA PUAN CEVABI (Band 6)

**"Trends in Urbanization Across Regions (2000-2020)"**

The line graph illustrates the percentage of urban population in four regions over a twenty-year period. Overall, all regions experienced an increase in urbanization, with Asia showing the most significant growth.

In 2000, Europe and the Americas had the highest urban populations at 72% and 78% respectively, while Asia and Africa had similar rates of approximately 35% and 36%. By 2020, the Americas maintained its lead with 84%, followed by Europe at 77%. However, Asia's urban population increased dramatically to 52%, representing a 17-percentage-point rise. Africa also grew to 44%, though at a slower pace than Asia.

Regions with already high urbanization rates, such as Europe and the Americas, showed modest growth. In contrast, developing regions like Asia experienced rapid urbanization during this period. This suggests that as countries develop economically, more people migrate to urban areas for employment opportunities.

### ✅ YÜKSEK PUAN CEVABI (Band 8-9)

**"Global Urbanization Patterns: A Regional Analysis (2000-2020)"**

The graph delineates urbanization trends across four distinct regions over a two-decade period, revealing divergent patterns of demographic shift. Overall, all regions demonstrated increased urbanization, yet the trajectories varied significantly, with Asia experiencing the most pronounced transformation.

In the initial period of 2000, the Americas and Europe already possessed substantial urban populations at 78% and 72% respectively, contrasting sharply with Asia and Africa, which hovered around the 35-36% mark. By 2020, this disparity had narrowed considerably in Asia's case, surging to 52%—a noteworthy 17-percentage-point increment—thereby outpacing Africa's more measured ascent to 44%. Conversely, the already-urbanized regions exhibited comparatively modest gains: the Americas reached 84%, while Europe's growth plateaued at 77%.

This dichotomy reflects broader socioeconomic patterns. Developed nations in Europe and the Americas, having undergone urbanization decades earlier, have largely plateaued, whereas burgeoning economies in Asia are experiencing accelerated rural-to-urban migration. Consequently, demographic forecasts suggest Asia's urbanization trajectory will continue its upward momentum, potentially converging with Western levels within the coming decades.

Technology has changed how people talk to each other. Some people think technology is bad for relationships. I think technology is good and bad.

First, technology helps people communicate. You can call your friends and family on phone or video chat. Social media lets you talk to many people. This is good for relationships.

### 🟡 ORTA PUAN CEVABI (Band 6-6.5)

**"The Impact of Technology on Human Relationships"**

While technology has undoubtedly transformed the way people communicate, I partially disagree with the notion that it has negatively affected human relationships. Although excessive technology use can be detrimental, technological advancements have also provided unprecedented opportunities for connection.

Proponents of the argument that technology harms relationships point to several valid concerns. Firstly, excessive screen time reduces face-to-face interactions, which are crucial for developing deep emotional bonds. Additionally, the rise of social media has created superficial connections, where quantity often supersedes quality. Young people, in particular, may neglect meaningful in-person relationships in favor of online interactions.

However, I contend that this perspective overlooks the benefits technology provides. For individuals separated by geographical distance—such as immigrants or long-distance couples—technology enables communication that would otherwise be impossible. Furthermore, online communities have allowed people with shared interests or experiences to connect meaningfully, providing support and companionship. Video calls and messaging platforms have made maintaining relationships easier for busy professionals.

In conclusion, while technology presents challenges to traditional relationship dynamics, it would be inaccurate to label it as entirely negative. The key lies in achieving a balanced approach, combining the convenience of digital communication with the irreplaceability of face-to-face interaction.

### ✅ YÜKSEK PUAN CEVABI (Band 8-9)

**"Technology and Human Relationships: A Nuanced Perspective"**

The assertion that technological advancement has predominantly undermined human relationships warrants careful examination. While excessive reliance on digital communication may indeed erode face-to-face interactions, the overall impact of technology on human connectivity is considerably more nuanced, and I largely disagree with this blanket condemnation.

Critics of technology rightfully highlight legitimate concerns regarding its deleterious effects on interpersonal relationships. Excessive screen time correlates with diminished capacity for sustained attention and authentic dialogue, particularly among adolescents. The proliferation of social media has engendered a phenomenon whereby quantity eclipses quality; individuals cultivate sprawling networks of superficial acquaintances whilst forgoing the cultivation of profound emotional bonds. Furthermore, the algorithmic curation of content perpetuates echo chambers, potentially fracturing communities along ideological lines.

Conversely, this perspective insufficiently acknowledges technology's transformative potential in facilitating meaningful human connection. For geographically dispersed populations—diaspora communities, long-distance relationships, and isolated individuals—technology has rendered previously unattainable connections accessible. Online communities have catalyzed the formation of bonds predicated on shared intellectual interests or lived experiences, transcending geographical limitations and fostering genuine camaraderie. Moreover, technology has democratized access to expert knowledge and support networks, enabling individuals with rare conditions or marginalized identities to find community and validation.

Crucially, the relationship between technology and human relationships is not inherently deterministic but contingent upon our usage patterns. Rather than attributing causality to technology itself, we must recognize that it merely amplifies pre-existing human tendencies—both prosocial and antisocial.

In conclusion, while technology poses legitimate challenges to traditional relationship dynamics, portraying it as categorically detrimental represents a reductive oversimplification. The empirical evidence suggests that when employed judiciously, technology fortifies rather than fractures human bonds, provided we cultivate intentional engagement practices.
